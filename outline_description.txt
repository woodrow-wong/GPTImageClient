Create a refined traditional Chinese baimiao line-art design intended as a practical drawing and color-planning reference for a handmade gourd artwork. It must be detailed enough for later painting and coloring, not reduced to an overly crude engraving stencil.

SUBJECT AND MOOD: Depict a mysterious young female flower-flame sorceress who appears briefly on an unnamed shore between reality and the other side. Her presence is quiet, restrained, solitary, and slightly melancholic rather than combative. She has very long black hair, narrow calm eyes, and an elegant fantasy appearance. The future color plan is black, deep wine red, dark violet, and restrained gold, but this output itself must remain black line art on white.

POSE AND GAZE: Show her as a full-length figure in a graceful three-quarter standing pose, placed slightly right of center. Her head is gently lowered and turned toward her open hand. Her gaze rests on a flower-shaped flame floating above her palm. One hand naturally holds a tall slender staff, nearly her full height; the other arm extends softly toward the center with palm facing upward. Her posture is composed and still. Long hair, translucent sleeves, and layered skirt move gently toward the side as if in a light wind. Her feet stand at the edge of shallow, mirror-like water.

FACE AND SILHOUETTE: Keep a refined feminine face with a clean jawline, delicate nose and lips, and expressive but restrained eyes. Preserve a strong, readable complete silhouette: wide hat, long hair, fitted upper dress, flowing layered skirt, extended arm, staff, and flower flame. Do not crop the head, hat, hands, staff, or lower dress.

HAT, HAIR, COSTUME, AND STAFF: Give her a large soft pointed witch hat with a broad, slightly undulating brim. Add one prominent fantasy flower ornament and a limited number of elegant vine or filigree lines around the hat; avoid dense bead-by-bead decoration. Her abundant hair falls below the knees in grouped flowing locks, with a few strands crossing the cheek and shoulders. Use broad hair masses plus selected interior strand lines, not thousands of individual hairs. Dress her in an off-shoulder dark fantasy gown with a fitted bodice, a defined waist, long translucent sleeves, and a sweeping multi-layer skirt with a light outer veil. Preserve clear boundaries among bodice, main skirt, veil, sleeves, skin, and hair so an artist can later assign colors. Add restrained floral-vine embroidery along the neckline, waist, sleeve edges, and selected skirt panels. The staff is slender and complete, topped by a circular branch-and-flower structure with a small central flame.

FLOWER FLAMES: The principal magical motif is a flower-shaped flame, combining the elegance of a red spider lily, cherry blossom, and lotus-like fantasy flower. Place the largest and clearest flower flame above her open palm. Add a smaller flower flame at the staff head and two or three small echoing flowers near the water. Express luminosity only through petal contours, radiating stamens, and sparse ripple lines; do not use tonal glow, gradients, blur, or dense sparkles.

COMPOSITION AND DEPTH: Use a near-square or gently vertical composition suitable for a curved gourd surface. The figure occupies roughly three quarters of the image height on the center-right. The staff creates a stable vertical line beside her. The extended hand and main flower flame connect the figure to the open area on the left. In the foreground, include only a few water ripples, one or two small flower flames, and a few drifting petals. In the middle ground, keep the figure, staff, and main flower flame dominant. In the background, suggest a distant unnamed shore with a few low horizontal contour lines and light mist-like curves. Keep generous white negative space and avoid visual congestion.

INSCRIPTION LAYOUT: Reserve a clean area on the left or upper-left for an elegant traditional inscription, preferably arranged in two or three balanced vertical columns. The inscription must not overlap the face, hat, staff, hands, or principal flower flame. It should feel like a quiet traditional colophon integrated with the illustration, not a modern poster title. Include exactly this Chinese text and no other writing: 在未名之岸短暂显现的花焰术士，游离于现实与彼岸之间，不解释来处，也不归属于任何一方

REFERENCE SYNTHESIS: Treat these as stable identity features derived from the reference set: the same long-black-haired woman; calm reddish eyes; broad pointed hat with a large flower ornament; dark floral gown; tall staff; flower flame above the palm; water and a distant otherworldly shore; restrained, detached, non-combat mood. Full-body references establish the standing pose, staff, open-palm gesture, water, hair, and dress. Close-up references establish the face, eyes, hat, flower ornament, earrings, and filigree character. Synthesize these observations into one coherent original design rather than copying any one poster.

OMIT: Do not reproduce any existing reference text, title, English wording, poem, signature, watermark, border, frame, divider, or poster layout. Omit large crystal shards, geometric grids, excessive stars, dense particles, photographic fog, water reflections, background texture, and repetitive tiny jewelry. Do not create a portrait crop. Do not add grayscale, color, fills, hatching, shadows, painterly marks, random characters, or any text other than the required inscription.

LINE-ART REQUIREMENTS: Use pure white background and crisp black line work. Maintain useful medium detail for later hand painting: strong primary contours, delicate facial and hand lines, grouped hair strands, clear garment layers, selected floral embroidery, readable flower structures, and lighter background contours. Use varied but controlled line weight and preserve closed or clearly bounded regions where practical for later coloring. The result should function both as a presentable baimiao artwork and as a reliable production drawing for an artisan.
